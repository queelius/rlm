\begin{frame}[t]
\frametitle{First, reproduce learning in a simpler setting.}
A recursive language model (RLM) can use code and ask helper models to solve parts of a problem. We eventually want to train those choices.
\medskip

\begin{block}{Today's question}
Can we use a published training recipe to improve a model's math answers on questions excluded from training?
\end{block}
\medskip
We use the authors' code for \textbf{Dr.\ GRPO}, a reinforcement learning (RL) method. It learns from scores assigned to the model's own answers.
\medskip

This is a small reproduction of an existing method. It establishes a reference for future work on recursive models.
\aside{Liu et al., \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20783}{\textit{Understanding R1-Zero-Like Training: A Critical Perspective}} (2025).\newline
\href{https://github.com/sail-sg/understand-r1-zero/tree/dfca49dd460ee7cc8e4a5a162c876a7fd6993b87}{Official code}; our run is much smaller than the paper's experiments.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[t]
\frametitle{Reward correct attempts, then change the model.}
\textbf{Illustrative question:} Solve $3x+2=14$. The correct answer is $x=4$.
\medskip

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.35}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
The model's attempts & Checker score & Learning direction \\
\midrule
Two answers end in $x=4$. & 1 each & Make these more likely. \\
Two end in $x=5$ or $x=6$. & 0 each & Make these less likely. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\medskip
Each attempt is compared with the average score for that question. These differences guide changes to the model's numerical settings, or \textbf{weights}.
\medskip

The checker judges the final answer. We do not supply worked solutions for the model to copy.
\aside{Simplified illustration with four attempts; our runs use eight. If every attempt receives the same score, this question supplies no relative learning signal.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[t]
\frametitle{We train on math, then test on different questions.}
Each training run used \textbf{Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B}, the same 512 math questions, eight attempts per question, and 32 weight updates on one A100 GPU.
\medskip

We tested the starting and trained models on the same \textbf{500 MATH500 questions}, all excluded from weight training.
\medskip

\begin{block}{We also tested two ways of asking each question.}
\textbf{Chat style:} Put the question inside a conversation, with an instruction to explain the steps and mark the final answer.\par\smallskip
\textbf{Question alone:} Give only the original question.
\end{block}
We trained once with chat-style input and twice with questions alone. Each run started from the original model.
\aside{Of the 500 questions, 64 were used to monitor progress. We did not choose the best checkpoint. Earlier pretraining exposure is unknown.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[t]
\frametitle{The input format changes the size of the gain.}
\textbf{Correct answers out of the same 500 MATH500 questions.}
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
Model tested & \shortstack{Chat-style\\input} & \shortstack{Question\\alone} \\
\midrule
Starting model & 154 & 305 \\
Trained with chat style & 308 & 317 \\
Trained with questions alone & 168 & 314 \\
Repeated question-only training & \RepeatChatScore & 306 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\medskip

Question-only training scored 314 and 306, versus 305 before training. We have not established a reliable gain over that stronger starting setup.
\aside{Each trained row is a separate run at its final checkpoint. The same checker and length limit apply throughout. Scores judge final answers, not every reasoning step.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[t]
\frametitle{A higher score does not always mean a new skill.}
\textbf{An actual test question:} Write $3/20$ as a decimal.
\medskip
\begin{block}{The saved responses, summarized}
\textbf{Before:} The model repeated the question until it hit the length limit.\par\smallskip
\textbf{After:} It explained the division and answered $0.15$.
\end{block}
\medskip
This came from the earlier 128-question chat-style test. Many gains were recoveries from repetitive responses, not necessarily new mathematical knowledge.
\medskip

\textbf{Next:} Test whether more training improves on the stronger starting setup. Then apply these checks when training an RLM's choices.
\aside{For future RLM work: compare against a strong starting setup and inspect what changes. Better math answers do not yet establish better problem decomposition.}
\end{frame}
