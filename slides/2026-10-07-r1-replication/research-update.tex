\begin{frame}[t]
\frametitle{Useful practice is neither all wrong nor all right.}
\textbf{Example:} Solve $3x+2=14$. Answer $x=4$ earns 1; $x=5$ earns 0.\\
Dr.\ GRPO compares rewards with their \textbf{same-question average}.
\smallskip
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Eight attempts on one problem & Scores & Relative signal \\
\midrule
All wrong & Eight zeros & None \\
Some right, some wrong & A mix of 1 and 0 & Learn from the contrast \\
All right & Eight ones & None \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\begin{block}{Why curriculum could help}
Practice problems the model can sometimes solve. Teach missing skills when needed, then increase difficulty as the model improves.
\end{block}
\aside{Contrast must occur within a question, not just across easy and hard questions. This concerns binary group-relative rewards, not all RL. Mixed scores do not guarantee learning. Related: \href{https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14476v1}{DAPO's filtering of uniform-reward groups}.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[t]
\frametitle{Existing skill needs a usable interface.}
\textbf{Our prompt check:} same two models, same 500 MATH500 questions.
\smallskip
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
How we ask & Before RL & After short chat-style RL \\
\midrule
Chat instructions & 154 / 500 & 308 / 500 \\
Question alone & 305 / 500 & 317 / 500 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\smallskip
\textbf{Lesson:} A poor interface can hide ability and inflate the apparent training gain. Test a capable starting setup.
\medskip

\textbf{RLM implication:} Before expecting good decomposition, show how to run Python, call a helper, pass the needed context, and read its real return.
\aside{SFT means learning by copying demonstrations; it is optional before RL. Our earlier RLM work already learned some executable routines, with limited transfer. The open question is what preparation enables flexible combinations, not whether we ever taught tool use.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[t]
\frametitle{Teach the interface, then practice useful choices.}
\textbf{Proposed curriculum:} build on skills the model can already demonstrate.
\medskip
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}

\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
\medskip
\textbf{Invented example:} Document 1 says ``Mira works at Aster Lab.'' Document 2 says ``Aster Lab is in Oxford.'' To find Mira's work city, pass \emph{Aster Lab} to the next lookup, then use its returned city: \emph{Oxford}.
\medskip

Use demonstrations or SFT where needed; then let RL practice alternatives. More attempts and modest temperature changes may reveal rare successes.
\aside{Hypothesis, not an established curriculum. Progressively remove help, but keep some earlier skills in practice. Verify actual tool returns; reward task success, not extra calls. Related: \href{https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24601v3}{RLM training}, \href{https://arxiv.org/html/2501.12948v1}{DeepSeek-R1 cold start}.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[t]
\frametitle{Prior work supports the ingredients, not our sequence.}
\begin{block}{DAPO: spend updates on informative attempts}
Its dynamic sampling removes groups that are all correct or all wrong. This directly connects to our reward-contrast lesson.
\end{block}
\smallskip
\textbf{Recursive Language Models:} The authors train a smaller model on filtered demonstrations of environment use and recursive calls.
\medskip

\textbf{DeepSeek-R1:} Uses supervised preparation before RL; R1-Zero does not. Preparation is a design choice, not a universal requirement.
\medskip

\textbf{Our next questions:} Which skills, teaching examples, exploration choices, and feedback make RLM learning work?
\aside{Sources: \href{https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14476v1}{DAPO \S3.2}; \href{https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24601v3}{RLM v3 \S3.2 and Appendix A}; \href{https://arxiv.org/html/2501.12948v1}{DeepSeek-R1 \S2.3.1}. These motivate an experiment; they do not establish our curriculum or its novelty.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[t]
\frametitle{A portfolio of small experiments, not one settled story.}
\begin{block}{First locate the failure; then change one thing.}
Curriculum is one promising idea. Missing skills, poor exploration, and misleading feedback need different remedies.
\end{block}
\smallskip
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.32\textwidth}p{0.59\textwidth}}
\toprule
Question & Small comparison \\
\midrule
Missing tool skills? & Prompt examples vs. demonstrations of tool use \\
Does teaching order help? & Same examples: staged vs. shuffled \\
Too little exploration? & More attempts or varied plans, same total budget \\
Can it use feedback? & Successful-only vs. error-and-repair examples \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\smallskip
\textbf{For each:} Check before/after RL; test new combinations, not just longer inputs.
\aside{Proposals, not results. Match compute and helper models. Track real execution, correct information passing, and final answers. Replicate promising effects; keep failures.}
\end{frame}
