\begin{frame}[plain]
\titlepage
\vfill
\begin{block}{In one sentence}
A 4-bit 8B model wrote a verified library for a new benchmark from three examples, and a 0.6B caller that never saw
the benchmark uses it correctly to 1M tokens; the useful capacity is in the proposer and in choosing evidence, not in
readers, recursion or larger controllers; and on whole-document reasoning (LongBench-v2) libraries stop helping.
\end{block}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[t]
\frametitle{One line of documentation decides whether an untrained caller uses a library.}
\begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
\begin{column}{.5\textwidth}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{}}
\toprule
MRCR, untrained 4B caller, docstring & Score \\
\midrule
Signature only & 0.18 \\
Description + signature (as the 8B wrote it) & 0.59 \\
Signature + usage line & 0.95 \\
Description + signature + usage line & 0.95 \\
\midrule
Hand-written library, usage line removed & 0.22 \\
Hand-written library, as written & 0.95 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{.46\textwidth}
\small
Usage line: \texttt{Typical use: FINAL\_TEXT(answer\_mrcr(request)).}\par\medskip
Without it the untrained 4B hands the whole input to a sub-model (overflowing at 128k and above) or retypes the
routine's output by hand.\par\medskip
\textbf{The distilled 0.6B scores 1.000 without the line; the untrained 0.6B fails either way (0.21, 0.13).}
Robustness comes from distillation, not size. The bootstrapper now inserts the line.
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\source{Ledger E25c, E25d; runs e25c-*, e25d-*. One family, one library, one seed per arm.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[t]
\frametitle{Boundaries, and what we withdrew.}
\begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
\begin{column}{.48\textwidth}
\begin{block}{LongBench-v2 (150 questions, chance 0.25)}
\footnotesize
Direct, truncated to 48k tokens: 0.367\\
Generic map-reduce library: 0.360 (9 times the tokens)\\
RLM without a library: 0.120\\[1mm]
Libraries pay where a task splits into local judgments; whole-document reasoning does not.
\end{block}
\footnotesize \textbf{Withdrawn:} ``the reader is distractor-limited''; length routing (pre-registered: the
ordering reversed on fresh questions).
\end{column}
\begin{column}{.48\textwidth}
\footnotesize
\textbf{Method lessons}\par\smallskip
\textbullet\ Verify under the semantics you run under: the kernel's \texttt{ask} raises on a cut-off reply,
scripted harnesses returned partial text (one routine crashed in 46 of 90 caller runs).\par\smallskip
\textbullet\ Verification must span deployment lengths: 8k-only teaching selected a pass-through routine that
scores 0 at 128k.\par\smallskip
\textbullet\ Routine evolution (GEPA-style): prompt rewrites by the 8B hurt; numbers helped (+0.048, ns);
random search found nothing.
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\source{Ledger E32, E31, E22b, E24, E24c. LongBench-v2: zai-org (Apache-2.0).}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[t]
\frametitle{What this changes, and what to test next.}
\small
\textbf{The thesis, sharpened.} For small recursive models, capability lives outside the weights: verified
routines (bootstrapped by a capable proposer, or distilled into a small one), documentation that a thin caller can
act on, and evidence selection inside the routine. Training pays where verification is local: in the proposer
and in the caller's call discipline.\par\medskip
\textbf{Next}
\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{1mm}
\item Second distillation round: more of the 8B's libraries plus the model's own verified successes, chat
included; two discovery seeds per arm.
\item Bootstrap MuSiQue on mixed-length teaching (running) and against the two-round reference.
\item Selection memory with pooled priors, since 30-question cells choose by noise.
\item A short note on program-solvable long-context benchmarks (MRCR; candidates: Michelangelo, RULER).
\end{enumerate}
\end{frame}
