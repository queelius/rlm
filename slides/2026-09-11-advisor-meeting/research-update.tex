\begin{frame}[t]
\frametitle{Can better handoffs make an RLM more reliable?}
\vspace{3mm}
{\large A follow-up to our earlier training experiments}\par
\vspace{5mm}
\begin{block}{Last time, we taught the model a basic routine.}
It learned to inspect a Python workspace and calculate an answer. Reliable use of helper model calls was still an open problem.
\end{block}
\vspace{2mm}
\begin{block}{This time, we studied the handoffs.}
Training improved a more demanding routine. We also found that the way helper answers are linked to their inputs can change accuracy substantially.
\end{block}
\smallnote{Alex Towell \quad | \quad Advisor discussion \quad | \quad Preliminary findings, 11 September 2026.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[t]
\frametitle{A long file can become many small questions\\whose answers Python can combine.}
A recursive language model (RLM) can work through a problem in steps, instead of trying to solve it in one model call.\par
\vspace{3mm}
\centering
\begin{tikzpicture}[font=\small]
\node[card,text width=3.0cm] (data) at (0,0) {A long file holds\\many users' questions.};
\node[card,text width=3.0cm] (root) at (4.8,0) {The main model\\inspects and divides.};
\node[card,text width=3.0cm] (helper) at (9.6,0) {Helpers decide:\\Is this about\\a place?};
\node[card,draw=Gold,fill=Gold!10,text width=5.0cm] (combine) at (4.8,-1.7) {Python combines the replies\\into totals for each user.};
\draw[flow,<->] (data)--(root);
\draw[flow] (root)--(helper);
\draw[flow,draw=Gold] (helper.south) |- (combine.east);
\draw[flow] (combine.north)--(root.south);
\end{tikzpicture}
\par\vspace{3mm}
\raggedright
The hope: solve an unfamiliar whole by combining familiar smaller steps.
\smallnote{The input stays in the workspace. Helpers can divide their work again; our tests mostly use one helper layer. RLMs are existing work. Our focus is making the handoffs reliable.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[t]
\frametitle{We changed how helper answers\\are linked to the text they describe.}
Suppose a helper reads: \emph{Maya bought a red bike.} It must judge two statements.\par
\vspace{3mm}
\begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
\begin{column}{0.47\textwidth}
\begin{block}{The input carries matching names.}
\texttt{k7ab}: Maya bought a vehicle.\par\medskip
\texttt{z2pm}: The bike is blue.
\end{block}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.47\textwidth}
\begin{block}{Each answer carries the same name.}
\texttt{k7ab}: Supported.\par\medskip
\texttt{z2pm}: Contradicted.
\end{block}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\vspace{4mm}
Without these names, the answers are matched only by their position in a list.
\par\vspace{3mm}
The software supplies the names. The model still has to choose the answers.
\smallnote{Simplified illustration. We changed the input and required output format, not the model's weights. These names are arbitrary identifiers, not hints about the correct answer.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[t]
\frametitle{Smaller calls were faster than large named calls\\in this local test.}
All methods answered the same 768 reading questions.\par
\vspace{2mm}
\centering
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
Method & Correct answers & Time \\
\midrule
48 questions per call, no names & 49\% & 19 seconds \\
48 questions per call, matching names & 85\% & 91 seconds \\
16 questions per call, no names & 81\% & 19 seconds \\
1 question per call, no names & 87\% & 31 seconds \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\par\vspace{2mm}
\raggedright
Single-question calls sent more input text. The best choice depends on which cost matters.
\smallnote{One Qwen3-4B test on one A100, with four simultaneous calls and no cache reuse. Times sum separate workload blocks and exclude server startup. All 848 calls returned valid answers. This is a local tradeoff, not a universal speed ranking. [E1]}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[t]
\frametitle{Proposed RLM change: manage record links\\and make helper group size an explicit choice.}
The surrounding program would keep track of records and answers, while the model chooses what to ask.\par
\vspace{4mm}
\centering
\begin{tikzpicture}[font=\small]
\node[card,text width=3.3cm] (a) at (0,0) {Choose a group size\\and keep each\\record's name.};
\node[card,text width=3.3cm] (b) at (4.8,0) {Ask helpers\\focused questions.};
\node[card,text width=3.3cm] (c) at (9.6,0) {Join replies by name\\and flag\\missing answers.};
\draw[flow] (a)--(b);
\draw[flow] (b)--(c);
\end{tikzpicture}
\par\vspace{5mm}
\raggedright
Test against the current RLM and smaller-call baselines, using the same models, tasks, and resource budget.
\par\vspace{3mm}
The result that matters is a more reliable \textbf{complete answer}.
\smallnote{Proposed component, not an established whole-RLM gain. Matching cannot guarantee correct reading, and identifiers are not a new invention. A later experiment could learn when to change group size.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[t]
\frametitle{We have a promising handoff result\\and a focused next research question.}
\begin{block}{What we know so far}
Worked examples improved one useful routine. Matching inputs to helper answers improved reading accuracy without retraining.
\end{block}
\vspace{2mm}
\begin{block}{What we want to establish next}
Can a change to the RLM's handoff procedure produce more reliable complete solutions at a useful cost?
\end{block}
\vspace{3mm}
{\large Would this be a worthwhile next study? What realistic task would make the test convincing?}
\smallnote{The larger goal remains learning how to divide unfamiliar problems into useful smaller ones. Today's evidence is narrower. End of the main talk; the remaining pages are optional discussion material.}
\end{frame}

\begin{tabular}{llrc}
\toprule
User & Question & Weight & Place question? \\
\midrule
