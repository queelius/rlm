# Early history: what survived the changes of direction

The strongest early results concern **teaching an executable routine, preserving record identity, and choosing a tractable view of the input**. They do not establish a general advantage from autonomous recursive decomposition. The record also contains real, bounded positive RL results; describing the whole period as “RL never worked” would be as misleading as promoting its favorable small panels to broad transfer.

The earliest recovered experimental checkpoint is August 24. It already contains a striking precursor to the later teaching question: supervision selected task-specific actions before the model could see the task. A corrected dispatcher solved its narrow benchmark, but that success did not transfer to model-to-model handoffs. Subsequent work repeatedly rediscovered the separation between local competence and usable whole-task behavior.

Entry points are the [initial presentation](../../slides/rlm-preliminary-results.tex), [September 11 evidence guide](../../slides/2026-09-11-advisor-meeting/evidence-and-methods.md), [detailed guide](../../slides/2026-09-11-advisor-meeting/detailed-findings-guide.md), [publication-options discussion](../../slides/2026-09-11-advisor-meeting/publication-options.md), and [results index](../RESEARCH_RESULTS.md). The publication document is historical positioning, not an independently verified novelty assessment.

## Keep four different accomplishments separate

| Accomplishment | What the early evidence actually tests |
|---|---|
| Tool competence | Emitting executable Python, inspecting a workspace, retaining state, and submitting an answer. The early 29/30 result and later MRCR retrieval gains belong here. |
| Programmed decomposition | A human-specified filter, fixed batch size, one helper per candidate, or an explicitly stated compound operation. The model may execute the routine; the experiment does not show discovery of it. |
| Helper competence | Classifying TREC/news/NLI records accurately and keeping each label attached to its record. Most large interface gains are component results. |
| Actual root-directed recursion | A sampled root chooses and executes child calls, receives their results, and completes its task. Several early RLM experiments do this, mostly at maximum depth one; their results do not establish learned recursive depth or a broadly useful learned stopping rule. |

## 1. August 24–29: narrow execution success, then a supervision and transfer failure

The ABI curriculum trained Qwen3-8B on 160 symbolic examples. On 30 tasks, direct answering scored 11/30, untrained RLM 0/30, and later SFT checkpoints 29/30. A separate benchmark-specific controller bootstrap reported direct 3/96 versus SFT-controlled RLM 96/96. These demonstrate that training can make the model use its execution interface. The changed supervision and harness prevent interpreting them as clean estimates of recursion's value.

The more informative result is the **v2a failure**. Its curriculum chose operation-specific first actions from a private task object, although the visible bootstrap turn exposed only a binding called `request`. The historical audit found one semantic bootstrap prompt mapping to all six operations, with conditional operation entropy 2.585 bits across 1,280 training rows. At steps 80, 160 and 240, the model succeeded on 8/48 screen tasks by favoring different single operations. Selected step 320 reached only 43/192 on the sealed test, with 82/96 counterfactual pairs failing; direct scored 12/192. Thus a positive aggregate gain concealed a task-conditioning failure.

**v2b repaired the identifiability problem** using the same generic dispatcher target, which reads the live payload and branches among the six operations. The selected step-36 model scored 192/192 on the sealed test, including all 96 counterfactual pairs; direct scored 9/192. Those 192 tasks are six operations over 32 groups, not 192 independent problem families. This is strong evidence for a learnable, input-conditioned dispatcher within that contract, not evidence of discovering decomposition.

The next **v3a workflow pilot** makes the boundary explicit. The selected checkpoint solved all 24 cases across four local workflows—dispatch, inspect/compute, stateful verification and repair—but 0/12 across delegation and batched delegation. There were six examples per workflow. The preceding v2b checkpoint scored 0/36 on this changed contract, with 34 failures. Perfect narrow performance had not bought general interface transfer.

The **v3b layout study** then produced a superficially favorable +15.3-point presentation interaction, but the varied-layout treatment lost 30.6 points on canonical tasks and increased model failures by 22.9 points. Its two training-seed interactions were +25.0 and +5.6 points. The renderer families were already used in training, and this was a development readout. The right conclusion is an unreliable tradeoff, not improved general robustness.

A better specified synthetic fixed-versus-adaptive study did show narrow success: four adapters, two optimization seeds, 18 unseen development context groups; 288/288 assigned-plan adherence in each arm, 144/144 visible answers in both, and conditional answers 134/144 fixed versus 144/144 adaptive. Adaptive routing used 93,586 versus 290,968 conditional helper-input tokens, a 67.8% reduction. The adaptive routine was explicitly taught: use Python for visible metadata or select a 16-record facet before classification. The frozen visible-answer gain gate failed at ceiling. Scenario test data remained unopened according to the recorded audit. The full qualification/training/screen/readout cost was 19.85 aggregate GPU-hours, not a cheap single inference ablation.

**Evidence checked:** the August 24 checkpoint; v2a/v2b sealed `summary.json`; v3a `pilot-result.json` and its family totals; v3b recovered report; adaptive study report and available analysis inventory. Native paths E01–E06 below.

## 2. September 8–9: a learned helper, correspondence failure, and the root bottleneck

TREC helper SFT is a defensible local learning result. On 489 normalized test groups, strict correct labels increased 355→473. The more semantic comparison is 374→473 canonical item correctness, or 376→473 when declared aliases are accepted: part of the strict gain repaired invalid arrays. The test result was much stronger than validation's 250/300, partly because the validation composition differed. This is one training seed with known local split exclusions; historical model pretraining exposure is unknown.

On six new 64-record context compositions, a fixed five-item classifier plus Python aggregation improved 1→15 exact counts out of 24 when the helper weights changed. The freely acting root improved only 4→6/24. These are not compute-matched harness conditions. More seriously, first-root responses differed before any helper call in 15/24 pairs despite matching requests and declared seeds, so the 4→6 result is not a clean child-mediated causal effect. The records remain useful evidence that competent child information can exist while the root fails to exploit it.

Trace examples distinguish failure modes: extending a list with characters of a JSON string, classifying only part of a dataset, emitting tool-looking text that never executes, and coincidentally correct counts from cancelling false positives and false negatives. Conversely, 22/23 fully covered composition episodes aggregated their retained labels faithfully. “The root always ignores helpers” is also false.

Long batches exposed a second weakness. Exact-cardinality constraints made all 24 long arrays valid while complete aggregate correctness stayed 0/24. The trained helper's aligned labels were 374/384 correct in the first quarter and 81/384 in the last. The full input was present and well within the model window. A singleton schema control kept every first-label decision identical across 1,536 calls but improved strict aggregate success 5→16/24 by removing surplus labels: a clean formatting effect, not improved classification.

Mixed-size training improved small-batch scores slightly and some long-batch aligned labels, but did not solve complete long output. Representation was more consequential: on four reused TREC validation contexts sampled twice, anonymous arrays scored 181/512 labels, indexed answers 405/512, and echoed-question answers 419/512. These changes bundle representation and constrained decoding. Rotation and input-scope controls subsequently narrowed the explanation: asking for a shorter output while leaving all 64 records visible barely helped (145→153/512), whereas exposing only the requested 16 scored 348/512; unchanged-weight repeats gave 143/153/349. The unit is four contexts, not 512 independent questions.

**Evidence checked:** the original helper audit through the cross-experiment dossier, and the detailed role/fixed-aggregation report with linked native audits. For the long-batch and representation suite this audit relies on the dossier's reconciled report-level evidence; it did not replay thousands of native calls. E07–E08.

## 3. September 9–11: correspondence replicates; meaningful root SFT emerges

The correspondence work became one of the clearest early results. Wrong visible-record IDs redirected labels: unrelated aliases scored 618/768 versus 298/768 for aliases pointing to another visible record. On 530 positions where the two records' gold labels differed, 361 responses matched the misleadingly named record and 110 the intended record. This supports a behavioral wrong-record explanation; it is not an internal attention or binding measurement.

Matching IDs also helped released models beyond the original trained helper. On the same 16-context MultiNLI panel at 64 records per call, Qwen accuracy was 43.8% without keys, 84.6% with row numbers and 83.4% with arbitrary matching tags. Mistral gave 34.7%, 58.1% and 52.0%. The direction replicated; Qwen's high absolute accuracy did not. Some Mistral arbitrary-tag outputs failed the complete-output contract and remain failures. Reverse/interleaved requested orders retained approximately 82%/81% versus 83% for original order on the same exposed contexts. Reordering is a robustness check on the same records, not new task transfer.

The most decision-relevant control was **equal total record work**, whose machine-readable counts were inspected directly here:

| Qwen policy, same 768 records | Correct | Workload seconds | Input / output tokens |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 48 records, no keys | 373 | 19.27 | 49,307 / 2,537 |
| 48 records, matching arbitrary keys | 651 | 91.04 | 60,768 / 15,228 |
| 16 records, no keys | 619 | 18.67 | 53,821 / 2,846 |
| One record per call | 665 | 31.08 | 154,594 / 4,076 |

The 848 calls were available and valid. Timing sums nonoverlapping policy workload blocks at concurrency four on one A100, excluding startup. Singletons were more accurate and faster than large keyed calls but repeated much more input. This defeats a universal “fewer calls means cheaper/faster” claim and makes small unkeyed calls essential baselines for any handoff proposal.

Root training also progressed beyond formatting. On one shared 72-task panel across eight newly selected context groups, faithful-and-correct execution was **12/72** for the already-trained fixed24 start, **55/72** after six updates on one demonstration corpus, and **53/72** after a separate six-update run on another corpus. Missing-outcome bounds are 12–19, 55–55 and 53–55. Both SFT realizations improved every context cluster. Reviewers read all 207 available programs and linked observations; their judgments were agent-authored and unblinded. These are two training realizations on one shared evaluation panel, not two independent tests of unseen-task generalization. The groups were new relative to named root inventories, not necessarily to helper training or every historical catalog.

On explicitly described, untrained combinations of familiar operations, strict answers improved 27→57/72. However, 48 gold answers were zero: faithful-and-correct counts were only **2→29/72**, with bounds 2–13 and 29–30. On 24 nonzero tasks they were 0→12, with two missing starting-policy outcomes. The effect survives that stricter check. This is the strongest early evidence that demonstrations taught better execution of compound instructions; the prompts themselves specified the two-stage calculation, so it is not autonomous plan discovery.

A supplied-plan diagnostic was equally informative in the opposite direction: original helper 0/8 exact totals, mixed-contract helper 1/8, known-correct local labels 8/8. Better local classifications had not yielded a reliable whole answer. Stable record handoffs and actual evidence consumption remained plausible next interventions, rather than established results.

## 4. RL: real learning, positive small panels, and repeated narrowing

The September 8 resumption note said no inspected RLM run had updated weights. That statement was true at its cutoff and became obsolete. Early campaigns had all-correct or all-wrong reward groups, unusable tool/client paths, or exporter rules that incorrectly rejected completed policy failures. A concrete recursive-call example elicited recursion in 5/6 original-weight tasks, whereas abstract hints yielded none in 168 episodes across three weights. This is why “cannot recurse” was too strong a diagnosis.

A later root-only pilot genuinely updated parameters, but validation was 2/8 before, 4/8 under unchanged weights, and 2/8 after. Sampling/service variation could match the putative learning effect. A continuation eventually made eight authenticated updates and improved **5→16/24** on six new root-composition contexts, with 13 wins and two losses. Its selected final checkpoint used five validation readouts on only two contexts; the helper had trained on the underlying question inventory. An independent training seed reached 15/24 from 9/24 on the same exposed contexts, with a selected step-six checkpoint. These are positive root-weight results worth retaining, but neither is broad task transfer, and token/call costs increased in some dimensions.

Broader, fixed-final 16-update training then gave **13→16/48** across 15 context groups, with eight wins and five losses. The main 64-record composition subset stayed **5→5/24**, and 256-record cases stayed **0/4**. There was no monotonic validation curve. Thus the larger, broader run did not establish the same useful gain as the favorable small panel.

By September 11, answer-only RL and an added authenticated-map calculation bonus were tested from the stronger QS6 root. The start had 57/72 exact answers; answer reward produced 55–57/72 after 19 updates, and the bonus 54/72 after 21 updates. Both had 576 planned opportunities, but admitted/optimized episodes and realized doses differed. The full faithful-and-correct primary endpoint was not reviewed; a nine-changed-path subset is not a population estimate. A numerical match to a calculation over a supplied map can reward wrong scope or coincidental agreement and cannot replace semantic execution verification.

After the meeting, one root RL update again failed to improve the shared 72-task panel (53 unchanged, 52 RL), while successful-trajectory SFT scored 57. A fresh-seed unchanged/SFT comparison was 55/57, with missing outcomes and mixed paired changes; the claimed compute saving did not repeat. These are [learning comparisons](../research-checkpoints/2026-09-12-learning-comparison.md), not a new independent dataset.

Helper RL has the clearest fresh-example attenuation. Eight updates on varied news examples gave **422→437/512** and **422→436/512** in two training seeds; matched-update SFT gave 427. On a newly frozen official-test panel the same models scored **422 base, 426 SFT, 427/429 RL**. Both RL-minus-SFT intervals included zero. Much of the gain was concentrated in science/technology; category losses remained. The two RL seeds agreeing on 511/512 earlier-panel labels is training-seed stability, not independent evidence across tasks. On DBpedia, the four models scored 209/208/209/210 out of 224. A whole-root news test stayed 3/16 with either helper although helper labels improved 103→108/128. See the [full checkpoint](../research-checkpoints/2026-09-12-broader-rl-learning-signal.md); the official-test counts were checked in machine-readable evidence here.

The defensible conclusion is **several real narrow RL changes, no established general or compute-efficient RL advantage**. Saturating reward groups, policy-induced admission censoring, terminal syntax, actual operator execution, and transfer must stay separate. E13–E15.

## 5. September 12–14: retrieval and selection expose useful nonrecursive work

MRCR supplied a useful tool-competence result: procedural SFT learned to find the requested assistant reply with Python. A fixed 32-update model scored 17/32 exact versus 2/32 before training on 16 held conversations sampled twice; 30/32 trained Python results contained the correct target. An output-preservation repair then recovered answers damaged by runtime whitespace stripping, producing 23/32. New longer conversations and third/fourth-occurrence readouts each gave 0→10/16 exact. These are same-family procedural transfer, with no helper calls.

Small final-token RL gains failed an early seed repeat (23→25/32 initially, 25→22 on repeat). A subsequently selected larger update did improve a fresh 32-conversation panel **24→27 exact**, with five wins and two losses. Both models still had 29/32 answers after ignoring edge whitespace and 29/32 clean targets in Python observations. The native evidence records zero child actions. Most improvement was answer delivery after retrieval, not better recursion or aggregate retrieval. This distinction survives even though one changed path did improve search. [Checkpoint](../research-checkpoints/2026-09-13-from-answer-delivery-to-information-selection.md); E16.

Generated record-selection tasks yielded a stronger environment-design lead. Python organized public updates into each candidate's current state without filtering candidates or deciding eligibility. A fresh 12-case panel sampled twice improved complete-set correctness **1→9/24**, at roughly half the input tokens, but every exact success was among six-candidate cases. A separate state-representation comparison gave raw tables 1/24, grouped unresolved records 4/24, and resolved current state 8/24. Grouping helped despite a longer input, so mere token reduction cannot fully explain it. These are different panels and must not be pooled.

Fixing the division at one helper per candidate then gave **4→10/12** in an initial six-case comparison. On twelve fresh cases sampled twice, it gave **10→22/24**, with twelve paired wins and no losses across seven cases; helpers made 302/304 individual decisions correctly. The native result confirms 304 helper calls and 207,004 input tokens versus 24 whole-input calls and 56,088 input tokens. This is a promising fixed decomposition on separable decisions at 3.7 times input cost. Python resolves history; the model applies eligibility. It is not learned planning or proof that singleton calls are always optimal.

Confidence-based refinement did not yet recover that gain cheaply: two least-confident refinements gave 12/24, first-two refinement 13/24, and analytical random-two expectation about 12.06/24. Many chosen-token log probabilities tied at zero. Decision-specific versus shared-reward training gave 8/24 and 7/24 against 6/24 unchanged, then 7/24 and 7/24 against 5/24 under new seeds on the same twelve cases. This is mixed evidence about learning a local decision, not additional new-case transfer. Earlier graded-selection RL changed list lengths and could improve reward while missing more eligible records. [Detailed checkpoint](../research-checkpoints/2026-09-13-decision-interfaces-and-record-grouping.md); E17.

The September 14 finite breadth screen was a response to these limits: direct, summary-helper and evidence-preserving helper arms across MuSiQue, FinQA, BoolQ, AG News and a length-admissible LongBench subset. Its [launch checkpoint](../research-checkpoints/2026-09-14-unattended-breadth.md) proves operation and defines the intended screen, not final scientific gains. Later campaign outcomes are outside this audit's cutoff.

## Persistent lessons and still-promising threads

1. **The information available before an action is part of the teaching target.** v2a's hidden operation is direct evidence; v2b's repair is a narrow positive control. Verify that each demonstrated action can be justified by the visible history, and distinguish a general dispatcher from task-specific code supplied through hidden knowledge. This early episode deserves a prominent place in any longitudinal account of later teaching repairs.
2. **Local correctness, correspondence, acquisition, reduction and final delivery need separate measurements.** TREC, supplied-label controls, compound zero-answer coincidences and MRCR whitespace failures show distinct bottlenecks. A final exact answer can hide cancelling errors; a wrong final can hide successful retrieval. The best early work actually inspected executed paths.
3. **SFT learned useful routines more consistently than it learned flexible orchestration.** Adaptive synthetic routing and faithful compound execution are real positives. v2b→v3a, v3b layout degradation and longer-context failures sharply limit their breadth. The unit of transfer should be a new task structure or decision requirement, not another seed of the same contexts.
4. **Small views and explicit record identity are reliable candidates, with measurable costs.** Matching keys repaired large-batch alignment across model families; smaller calls remained strong baselines. Candidate-local decomposition later gave a large fresh-case effect. No one policy dominated correctness, prompt volume, output volume and latency.
5. **A genuine optimizer update and a same-panel gain are necessary but insufficient evidence for useful RL.** The record includes positive root and helper effects, but broader transfer, additional-SFT controls and repeats repeatedly weakened the larger claim. Preserve the positive and negative endpoints together, including missingness and dose.

Three neglected or unfinished early questions remain worth checking against later work before starting anything new:

- **End-to-end record handoffs under a fixed budget.** The correspondence signal is unusually well localized and replicated. The missing result was a complete-task improvement from verified record joins and actual evidence consumption, compared with simple smaller batches. A new tag-only component study would not close that gap.
- **Learning which representation or scope a decision needs.** Public-state organization and candidate-local decisions provide concrete successful actions to choose among. The smallest informative comparison would hold helper budget fixed and compare learned selection with fixed grouping and random refinement; current confidence signals were weak. This is a proposed continuation, not an observed adaptive policy.
- **Transfer of an observable execution routine with faithful nonzero endpoints.** The QS6 compound result is stronger than its headline exact score implies after auditing, yet narrow. A controlled new-family test of visible evidence acquisition, correct operator/scope and explicit result return would discriminate reusable procedure from familiar-template following more directly than another dose sweep.

These are historical opportunities, not claims of novelty or instructions to launch. Later work may already address them; the parent audit should reconcile that before ranking current experiments.
