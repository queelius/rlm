# What changed overnight

The strongest lead is now more specific: **we can make existing worked examples
more useful by changing what the model has seen before each answer, without
changing the answers it is trained to produce.** This helped again with another
training seed and on additional crafting goals. It is a promising research result,
not yet a general method validated across domains.

The GPU has moved on to checking RL on different goals. The earlier reward-based
training gains are real measurements, but are still confined to familiar goals
at this cutoff. More runs are not automatically stronger evidence; the next runs
need to challenge these two findings.

## 1. The teaching change reproduced

We train the model to produce the next tool action from a worked example. The
original example-making program knows the recipes in advance. It sometimes asks
about a part before the interaction has introduced that part. The repaired
examples look up recipes in an order that makes each requested name available
first. They still use the original list of correct actions; they are not an
independent planner that discovers the solution from scratch.

For intuition, imagine the goal is a pickaxe and one part is called **part Z**.
An example that asks about part Z before explaining why it matters gives the
learner less context. Looking up the pickaxe first can reveal part Z, making
the later request understandable. This is a simplified illustration, not a
literal task from the dataset or proof of the sole cause of the improvement.

| Fixed comparison | Original examples | Repaired examples | Discovery-based reference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Initial pilot | 1/32 | 14/32 | 14/32 |
| Another training seed, same goals | 2/32 | 13/32 | 13/32 |
| Eight additional goals | 3/32 | 17/32 | 15/32 |

Each row repeats eight goals across two recipe settings and two attempts.
The two added rows cover **16 goal identities**, not 64 independent problems.
Together, repaired examples give 30/64 successes versus 5/64 originally:
26 paired improvements, one regression, and 37 unchanged outcomes.
The task-grouped exploratory interval for the difference is 21.88–57.81
percentage points. Related recipes still make tasks dependent.

A different, randomized repair also helped: 27/64 on the added measurements.
Both repairs use the same 366 answer strings, all 23 training batches' answer
tokens and their normalization, and the recorded starting weights of their
matched original teacher. Input histories, their length and computation differ.
This rules out several simple training-dose explanations, not every mechanism.
Neither repair has demonstrated superiority to the discovery-based reference.

These goals had been examined in the broader study. This is useful replication,
not a final untouched test. All 40 cells in the combined teaching/dose/Phi
analysis have native audits: 576 attempted episodes, no unknown outcomes or
transport failures. That count describes the evidence catalog, not 576
independent problems or 576 replication attempts.

Evidence and exact controls: [teaching synthesis](teaching_synthesis_20260929/FINDING.md),
[portable data](teaching_synthesis_20260929/FINDING.json).

## 2. More training partly rescues the original examples

The original teacher improved from 1/32 to 8/32 with twice the training, and
remained at 8/32 with three times the training. The discovery teacher went
14 → 15 → 17 out of 32 at the same fixed endpoints. We report all endpoints,
not whichever checkpoint worked best in each recipe setting.

This changes the interpretation: **the original examples are not unlearnable.**
The repair seems to help learning at the tested budgets. We have not shown an
advantage after unlimited training, and the direct repair-versus-longer-training
comparison is less precise than repair-versus-matched-training.

The second model, Phi-4-mini, also favored the original discovery package:
4/16 versus 1/16 without ingredient assistance. But we have not yet trained
Phi on the repaired examples. That is the next direct test of whether the
repair method transfers between model families.

## 3. RL improved familiar tasks; transfer is being tested now

The completed comparison holds the execution interface fixed within each column:

| Model weights | Model supplies ingredients | Code fills already-known ingredients |
|---|---:|---:|
| Before reward-based training | 9/16 | 14/16 |
| One update trained without assistance | 13/16 | 15/16 |
| One update trained with assistance | 12/16 | 16/16 |

These are eight goals used in RL training, with different attempt seeds at
evaluation. We have one training batch/update per method. Calls and output
tokens increased; malformed commands and repeated mistakes did not disappear.
This is not yet proof of efficient planning, transfer, or an advantage over
additional supervised training. The queued new-goal and extra-example controls
are designed to distinguish those possibilities.

## 4. What we are not expanding

- **A remaining-work table did not rescue deeper tasks.** Both the calculated
  table and its matched control solved 0/8 attempts. It reduced some unnecessary
  lookups, but repeated impossible crafts remained and output cost increased.
  Merely displaying the right arithmetic is not enough in this small test.
- **The household-game signal is too narrow.** Writing the actual command after
  SFT solved 4/24 attempts versus 0/24 before training, but all four wins come
  from two games in one scene. Most failures involved commands not currently
  allowed. Shorter failed episodes are not greater efficiency.
- **Helpers have done real work, but their value is still unknown.** The short
  delegation screens created children, including one successful subtask. All
  parent tasks were interrupted by the screening limit. We will compare whole
  task completion under the original shared budget before adding more recursion.

Evidence, examples and limitations: [harness synthesis](harness_synthesis_20260929/README.md).

## Next decisions and a possible paper

The clearest paper candidate is a controlled study of **repairing existing
demonstrations so their decisions have usable context**. The practical attraction
is reusing correct answers rather than collecting more answer labels. This needs
model-family transfer, a prospectively fixed fresh-goal test, and a careful
comparison with prior work before making a novelty claim.

The general teacher–learner information mismatch is already studied, including
in [Guided Policy Optimization](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15418v2).
Our candidate contribution is the particular executable repair and its strong
answer/batch controls, not the discovery that an informed teacher can be hard
to imitate. The [literature decision memo](literature_decisions_20260929/README.md)
also explains why we are not immediately importing a larger RL training system.

Priorities are: test the repair in Phi; finish the already accepted RL-transfer
and fresh-training controls; determine whether one helper improves whole-task
success. If errors persist after these, targeted teaching on states the model
actually reaches may be more informative than another reward formula. That last
idea remains conditional, not a queued result.
